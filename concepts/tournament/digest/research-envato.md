# Envato Elements license (Dec 8, 2025 revision) as applied to assets shipped inside a paid iOS app, plus a sourced shortlist of Envato Elements items for a vibrant, bold-outline cartoon iOS app/game

Envato Elements has one license for every stock item on the platform (no standard/extended tiers, identical across Core/Plus/Ultimate plans), last revised 8 December 2025. A license is created automatically at download; since January 2026 no project "registration" is required, though you can name/label licenses per project. Each license covers one project to create one End Product, and Envato's FAQ says explicitly that a single license is enough for one software/SaaS product as long as the item is fully integrated and users are not given access to the underlying item; a second app needs a second license. The license only becomes perpetual if the End Product is completed while the subscription is active; if you cancel before it is done, the license terminates. After cancellation you can keep shipping the finished app and make minor updates, but you cannot use downloaded items in new or materially different products, and you must uninstall fonts.

The big catch for an iOS app is fonts. Fonts are licensed per device (two computers per seat, subscriber only), and clause 13(d)(ii) says you cannot "incorporate or distribute the Font or Add-on within an End Product"; you may only distribute text or designs created with the font. The only carve-out is a "web-enabled Font" embedded in an End Product that does not let users extract it or create new text with it, which is written for websites, not app bundles. Because iOS custom fonts ship as TTF/OTF files inside the .ipa and are typically used to render user-entered text, bundling Envato fonts in the app is not clearly permitted. Safe use: app icon, logo/wordmark, marketing screenshots, and pre-rendered title art (rasterized or converted to outlines); use SIL OFL/Google fonts for runtime text.

UI kits, icons, illustrations, stickers, speech bubbles, textures and sound effects are fine inside a paid app with an active subscription, subject to: no on-demand/"build it yourself" features where end users customize Envato items into their own product (clause 12(c)); items must not be extractable from the End Product (12(e)); no trademark claims over an item (12(f)); and you must check third-party components bundled in kits (e.g., Google Material Icons, free fonts). Sound effects may even be broadcast; Envato music may not be broadcast or used as standalone tracks, though that is moot if all music comes from Suno. Apple's App Review 5.2.1 separately requires that you own or have licensed everything in the app, so keep named license certificates per app.

The shortlist below (24 items, all verified live on elements.envato.com) covers iOS/game UI kits, modular character-creation kits for building an original ensemble cast, comic speech bubbles and halftone textures for the pop/comic energy, sticker and emoji vectors, five playful comic display fonts (for icon/marketing use), cartoon and game-UI sound packs, and Xcode-ready app-icon templates.

## Implications for the concept
- Treat the whole app as ONE End Product: create one named license per Envato item for 'App v1' (use the 'Always ask' preference so every download is labeled), and keep the subscription active until the 1.0 build that ships is 'complete'. A sequel, companion app, or materially different product needs fresh licenses and an active subscription; ordinary updates to the same app do not.
- Do not bundle Envato TTF/OTF/WOFF files in the Xcode project (UIAppFonts / 'Fonts provided by application'). Clause 13(d)(ii) forbids distributing the font inside an End Product, and 13(c)'s web-font exception explicitly excludes products that let users 'create new text using it', which any text field styled with the font would do. Use Envato display fonts only for the app icon, logo/wordmark, App Store screenshots, and static title art exported as PNG/SVG outlines; pick SIL OFL fonts (e.g., Google Fonts comic/rounded faces) for runtime text so the vibrant cartoon type still ships legally.
- Because the concept is meant to be personal and daily, any 'make your own avatar / sticker / meme / card' feature built from Envato parts risks clause 12(c) (on-demand / build-it-yourself) and the FAQ's ban on end users manipulating the End Product. Use Envato character-creation kits to author a FIXED original cast and fixed stickers offline; if users must customize things, build that feature from original or commissioned art, or from Suno/your own generated content, not from Envato items.
- Ship Envato graphics through Xcode asset catalogs (compiled into Assets.car) and audio as compressed bundled resources; never include PSD/AI/EPS/Sketch sources in the bundle. That is the 'reasonable steps' the license expects against extraction, and it also keeps the .ipa small.
- Sound effects are the least constrained Envato category (commercial, even broadcast, allowed), so a rich cartoon/UI SFX layer can come entirely from Envato while Suno supplies all music. If any Envato music is ever added, it must be synced to a larger experience, never exposed as a standalone playable track or 'jukebox' item; the same standalone-track caution should be checked against Suno's own terms for the in-app song library.
- The blockbuster/superhero energy must come from original characters: skip or heavily rework any Envato item whose art or tags echo Marvel/DC/Superman (e.g., the Superhero Mascot pack), since Apple 5.2.1 and 4.1 make you liable for third-party IP and copycat likenesses regardless of Envato's license.
- Make the app name/logo mark original artwork (or ensure any trademark filing expressly excludes the Envato item), because clause 12(f) and the logo FAQ bar trademark claims over an Envato item embedded in the logo.
- Open each kit's download package before committing to it: several shortlisted kits bundle third-party components (Google Material Icons in the Mobile Design System, 'free fonts' in the UI kits) that carry their own licenses under clauses 16-18.
- Budget: the Core plan ($16.50/mo annual) carries the identical lifetime commercial license as higher tiers; there is no extended license to buy. Higher tiers only add Envato AI credits, which the concept does not need because music comes from Suno.
- Keep an audit trail for App Review and any IP dispute: download and archive the license certificate PDF for every item under the app's project name, plus screenshots of each item page, since authors can remove items later.

## Open uncertainties
- Whether an iOS app could ever qualify under clause 13(c)'s 'web-enabled Font as part of an End Product' carve-out (e.g., bundling the WOFF and only rendering fixed, non-editable strings) is not addressed anywhere Envato publishes; the conservative reading is that app bundling is prohibited. Written confirmation from terms@help.envato.com (subject 'Envato Elements License') is recommended before any font file ships in the binary.
- Envato gives no iOS-specific guidance on what counts as 'extractable': files inside an .ipa can be unzipped by a determined user even when compiled into asset catalogs, so 'reasonable steps' is a judgment call.
- The boundary between an allowed in-app 'theme picker' and a prohibited 'build it yourself' customization feature (clause 12(c)) is undefined for apps; only t-shirt/print examples are given.
- When a continuously updated app counts as 'completed' is only partially defined: the FAQ allows minor updates after cancellation but says new or materially different products need a new license and active subscription; where a large feature update falls is unclear.
- Envato's help-center pages block automated fetching (HTTP 403), and the relevant Envato forum threads on fonts in apps and on video-game use now return 503 or redirect to the author help center, so no Envato staff answer specific to app/game bundling was captured; the license text above was obtained via a secondary renderer and matches the search snippets, but should be re-read on the live page before relying on clause numbers.
- Reddit was not reachable through the search index and the social-listening search returned only one tangential 2023 post, so there is no community evidence about Envato asset use in shipped App Store apps.
- The 25 February 2026 Gen AI limit change is sourced only from a search snippet of the Envato help article (page blocked); it does not affect stock-item licensing but the exact figures should be verified.
- Apple's own documentation on bundling custom fonts (UIAppFonts) is JavaScript-rendered and could not be quoted; the statement that iOS custom fonts ship as font files inside the app bundle is standard platform behaviour but is not cited to a fetched page here.
- Item pages were verified live on 24 September 2026, but Envato authors can withdraw items at any time; a withdrawn item stays licensed for completed End Products but cannot be re-downloaded, so download and archive chosen items early.
- Suno's own commercial terms for distributing generated music inside a paid app and as an in-app 'song library' were out of scope for this Envato task and need separate verification.
