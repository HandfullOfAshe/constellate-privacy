# Suno commercial-use terms, legal status, and production facts for embedding Suno-generated music in a paid iOS app (as of 24 Sept 2026)

Suno's current Terms of Service (last revised 10 Aug 2026, effective 3 Sept 2026) say that for Pro and Premier subscribers "Suno hereby assigns to you all of its right, title and interest in and to any Output," subject to the Terms, but commercial exploitation is only allowed for Output you obtained via a "permitted download" on a paid tier. Free-tier output is personal, non-commercial only, and upgrading later does not retroactively license it. Suno also disclaims that any copyright will vest in Output, and US law (Copyright Office Part 2 report, Jan 2025; Supreme Court cert denial in Thaler v. Perlmutter, 2 Mar 2026) means purely prompt-generated audio is not copyrightable; only human-authored layers (your lyrics, your Studio arrangement) are. No attribution is required on paid plans, though Suno reserves the right to publicly identify Output as AI-generated and embeds tier/download fingerprints you may not remove.

The big operational change is downloads. Since 3 Sept 2026 (announced in Suno's blog on 10 Aug 2026 after the Warner settlement), Free gets 7 lifetime non-commercial trial downloads, Pro 20 downloads/month, Premier 60/month, with unlimited downloads from Suno Studio for Premier only. One song = one download regardless of format or stems; limits apply to the whole back catalogue; extra downloads cost about $2.99 each. Because commercial rights attach to the download, a library of a few hundred tracks is practically a Premier-plus-Studio job. Pricing: Pro is widely reported at $10/mo ($8 annual) and Premier $30/mo ($24 annual); suno.com/pricing rendered $8/$24 during this research, so verify on checkout. There is no public API (partner-only intake opened July 2026), so music must be pre-generated and bundled; that fits a local-first app.

Legally the ground is still moving. Warner (25 Nov 2025), BMG (12 Aug 2026) and Believe/TuneCore (8 Sept 2026) settled and licensed; Suno launched the v6 family on 9 Sept 2026 (v6 and v6-wild paid-only, v6-mini free), retiring all prior models. But Sony and UMG filed a second suit on 18-19 Sept 2026 (D. Mass., seeking up to $9B over 60,202 recordings, arguing v6 is "fruit of the poisoned tree"); a Munich court ruled against Suno for GEMA on 31 July 2026; an indie-artist class action survived dismissal; Jason Isbell filed a right-of-publicity class action on 31 Aug 2026; and a 55M-user data breach surfaced in July 2026. Apple's App Review Guidelines contain no rule on AI-generated bundled audio; what applies is 5.2.1 (you must hold the rights), 5.2.2 (comply with the third party's terms), 4.3(b) (no low-effort clones, strengthened 8 June 2026), 5.1.2(i) (disclose sharing personal data with third-party AI, irrelevant if nothing is generated at runtime), and 1.2 only if users share content. Apple Music separately launched AI Transparency Tags (Mar 2026). Production facts: v6 generates up to 8 minutes; style prompt 1,000 chars, lyrics 5,000 chars with [Verse]/[Chorus]/[Instrumental]/[End] meta tags; Instrumental toggle plus Exclude field; Personas, Covers, Extend, Replace Section, Custom Models (6+ tracks), Voices (own voice only), Sounds mode for BPM/key-locked loops and one-shots, WAV export, 12-stem Auto Split and ~100-instrument Advanced Split (Premier), MIDI extraction. For loops, prompt "seamless loop, no intro, no outro, exactly N BPM", then trim at bar boundaries; on iOS ship PCM/CAF or loop via AVAudioEngine because AAC priming frames break gapless playback.

## Implications for the concept
- Budget the music library around downloads, not credits. Commercial rights attach only to a 'permitted download' on a paid plan: Pro yields 20 commercially usable tracks/month, Premier 60/month, and Premier's Studio downloads are unlimited. For a launch library of 100-300 tracks, Premier ($30/mo, or $24 annual) plus Studio is the practical tier; extra downloads at ~$2.99 each are a fallback. Never generate anything for the app on the free tier; it can never be licensed retroactively.
- Music can only be pre-generated and bundled (no public Suno API; unofficial wrappers breach the ToS). That matches the client's local-first, no-server preference and avoids Apple 5.1.2(i) third-party-AI disclosures entirely. Design the concept around a curated, hand-finished library (themes, character songs, daily/occasion tracks, adaptive layers) rather than runtime generation.
- Stack human authorship on top of Suno output so the app's music has a defensible copyright layer: write every lyric yourself, arrange/edit in Suno Studio, layer stems, and log creation date, tier, and download date per track. Purely prompt-generated audio is not copyrightable in the US, so competitors could legally copy raw Suno tracks; your lyrics, arrangements, characters and world are what you actually own.
- Apple review: there is no AI-audio rule, but 5.2.1/5.2.2 mean you must be able to show you hold the rights and complied with Suno's terms on request. Keep a rights ledger (Suno account tier, ToS version, download receipts, lyric authorship) and mention in App Review notes that music was generated under a Suno Pro/Premier commercial license with original lyrics. Suno's embedded fingerprint stays in the files and is harmless in an app.
- Do not build anything that lets users generate, remix, or share music inside the app. That would (a) make it a 'competing music-generation product' under Suno's ToS, (b) trigger Apple 1.2 UGC moderation requirements and 1.2.1 creator-app age gating, and (c) require third-party-AI data disclosures. Keep music a developer-authored feature.
- Match the pop-culture 'tone' with original characters and voices only: Suno's ToS bars voice models of other people and impersonation, Spotify/Apple Music ban AI voice clones, and the Isbell right-of-publicity suit targets artist-identity mimicry. Use Personas/Voices built from Suno's own generated singers (or the client's own voice) to give each original character a consistent signature sound; never prompt real artist or show names.
- Give music a structural role the tools actually support: Sounds mode for BPM/key-locked loops and one-shot SFX; Auto/Advanced Split stems for calm/medium/intense adaptive layers; section editing and Extend for variants; Covers to render one motif across genres for different moods or 'occasions'; Custom Models (6+ tracks) to lock a house style so the library feels like one composer.
- Engineer for gapless playback and app size: export WAV from Studio, trim loops at bar boundaries, then ship as ALAC-in-CAF or PCM and loop with AVAudioEngine (AAC priming frames break seamless loops). WAV is ~10 MB per stereo minute at 44.1 kHz, so use On-Demand Resources or app thinning for a large library.
- Treat the Suno legal situation as a live risk and keep optionality: the Sept 2026 Sony/UMG suit seeks an injunction-scale remedy against v6, the GEMA ruling could constrain EU availability, and Suno tightened terms once already in 2026. Keep all masters and stems offline, avoid over-indexing the concept on any one vendor, and consider a secondary source (Epidemic Sound or human composers) for a few marquee tracks. A soundtrack-sale or streaming release of the app's music is allowed on paid tiers but must carry DDEX AI disclosures via the distributor.
- Attribution is optional; a tasteful 'Original music created with Suno' credit in the app's About screen is good practice and consistent with Suno's stated right to identify output as AI-generated, without cluttering the UI.

## Open uncertainties
- Pricing: suno.com/pricing rendered Pro $8/month and Premier $24/month with no promo banner, while every secondary source (and Suno's own game-dev-oriented pages) cite $10/$30 monthly and $8/$24 annual. The page may have defaulted to annual billing; confirm at checkout.
- Ownership wording is inconsistent across Suno's documents: the ToS 'assigns all right, title and interest' to Pro/Premier users, help articles say paid users 'own' songs, but Digital Music News (Dec 2025) quoted a since-revised help-center line saying users 'generally are not considered the owner.' The ToS is the controlling document; treat 'ownership' as a contractual grant, not a copyright.
- Whether extra purchased downloads on a Free account carry commercial rights is unconfirmed; only paid-tier downloads are explicitly commercial.
- The 8-minute maximum for v6 comes from secondary guides; Suno's own 'How long will my song be?' help article returned 404 during research.
- Sounds credit cost (reported as 2 credits) and whether Sounds/loops output is covered by the same download counting rules are not stated in Suno's primary docs.
- Whether stems exported via Studio (Premier) fall entirely outside the monthly cap is stated by Suno's FAQ ('without limitation'), but the ToS's 'permitted download' language has not been litigated; keep records either way.
- The Sept 2026 Sony/UMG suit is at the complaint stage; an adverse ruling or injunction against v6 could change model availability or terms again. GEMA's Munich judgment is under likely appeal and its territorial effect on Suno's EU service is unclear.
- Reddit threads could not be retrieved (reddit.com blocks this agent's fetcher), so community reaction to the Sept 3 download caps and first-hand reports of Suno music in App Store apps are not directly sourced here.
- Apple has no published precedent on AI-generated bundled music; reviewer behavior under 5.2.1 is anecdotal. Third-party blogs claim Apple requires an 'AI-generated' disclosure; no such text exists in the guidelines or App Store Connect questionnaire as of the June 8, 2026 revision.
- Suno's fingerprint/watermark is described in the ToS but the technical scheme (and whether Apple, DistroKid, or detection vendors read it) is not documented.
